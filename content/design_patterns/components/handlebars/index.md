# Handlebars


## Bullmoose

The bullmoose handlebar type was first invented by Tom Ritchey in 1978.
The functional motivation was a one-piece bar-and-stem so
that [The handlebar could not slip within the stem's grip](https://youtu.be/CAQ2pyuxQto?t=734).
But Ritchey does not just build bikes; he builds works of mechanical
art. The bullmoose is beautiful to look at with a clean look
with thin tubes. They come wide with generous sweep back.

Another nice detail is that with the bullmoose the rise is all in the
stems, not the main crossbeam.  Higher handlebars are one of things
that lead to comform (and even hardcore gravity riders are getting
into more rise on the handlebars these days for performance reasons
not comfort). Functionally, that's great but aesteticly, not some much
IMHO. The real solution would to design a taller head tube but then
"Dood, how am I gunna slam my stem so folks know I'm hardcore?"

When doing drop to flat bar conversions, a repurposed quill stem
designed for dropbars often looks awkward with a flatbar; the unified
stem-and-bar that is the bullmoose does not suffer from that.


## Grips

- Paddle type
  - [PDW's Cork Chop Grips](https://ridepdw.com/products/cork-chop-grips) are my personal favorite, but some folks just do not like the paddle style
  - [Ergon paddle grips](https://ergonbike.shop/collections/grips) are very nice 
  - If going for the rubberized two-finger bar end type make sure it adjusts independently from the main paddle grip
- Tubular type
  - [Oury](https://www.lizardskins.com/oury)
  - [ESI silicon foam grips](https://esigrips.com/?srsltid=AfmBOoqNSNk8Ta8pSFXgLX9o0hyK-k18JXdF8GPbpHT_jSWFFP_rmZTJ)
    are excellent for comfort (someone called them shock absorbers for the hands :), but not the most durable material,
    cheap to replace so that migth just be worth it if you are dealing with wrist pain
